Help and support

The questions that come up most, and how to reach us.

Contact us

Write to contact@replere.app. Include your iPhone model and iOS version: that alone solves half of all problems.

Are you a sophrologist?

Replere features sessions recorded by certified sophrologists, with their name, certification and city. To offer yours, write to sophrologues@replere.app with your certification, your practice and the session you have in mind (purpose, length).

Pricing

Does Replere cost money?

The essentials are free: all sessions (and they always will be), the daily path with its phrase of the day, The basics program, the music of your week, your streak and the sophrologist map. No ads.

Replere Plus, an optional subscription, adds the memory and reflection exercises, the other programs, the argument coach, your data in detail (including the recovery curve) and your session history. The price is shown in the app before any purchase, and you can cancel the subscription at any time from your Apple account settings.

Your data

Where is my data?

On your phone. We have no server that stores your sessions, so we can't see them. The one exception, if you connect Garmin: Garmin only sends workouts to a server, so a Replere server in France receives them, passes them to your iPhone, then deletes them (see the privacy policy).

How do I export or delete everything?

Settings → Profile: “Export my data” creates a readable file, “Delete my account” erases everything, immediately and permanently. Nothing to request from us.

The app

Is there a limit on sessions?

No. All sessions are free to use, with no daily limit, including the summary and tracking.

The app doesn't detect my workouts

Check that Apple Health access is granted: iOS Settings → Health → Data Access → Replere. The app reads when your workouts end, their type, and your heart rate.

“Near me” doesn't find anything

Either location access is turned off (iOS Settings → Replere → Location), or no practice is listed nearby. You can always type an address by hand.

What the research says

We'd rather be accurate than salesy. Here is what is established, and what isn't.

What is well documented: the mechanisms

  • Slow breathing after exercise. A randomized crossover trial by Sugimoto et al. (2025, Clinical Physiology and Functional Imaging, 15 healthy young men) found that breathing at six cycles per minute speeds up parasympathetic reactivation after exercise, with higher heart rate variability than free breathing. A small sample: a signal, not definitive proof. Reference
  • Progressive muscle relaxation and sleep. A meta-analysis of 31 randomized controlled trials, 2,277 participants, found a large effect on sleep quality. Reference
  • In athletes. A randomized trial in athletes aged 18 to 36 with sleep problems reports a large effect (Cohen's d = 1.44).

What is much less so: sophrology as a discipline

Studies of sophrology itself are few and small: childhood asthma, anxiety in general practice, fibromyalgia. The results are mixed.

The SOPHROCARE trial, in young people with congenital heart disease, showed no difference on its primary outcome, cardiorespiratory fitness at twelve months. We mention it because it would be dishonest to cite only the favorable results. Reference

In other words: the tools the sessions use (slow breathing, progressive relaxation, body awareness) have measured, replicated effects. The “sophrology” label adds no further evidence. Replere is not a treatment, and none of these studies show that it can replace one.
