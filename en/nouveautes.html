What's changing in October

Testers told me two things. The voice went too fast. And without a watch, you couldn't see anything. Here is what I changed.

Anatole, October 26, 2026

Your pulse, even without a watch

Before the session, you rest your index finger on the rear camera for thirty seconds. The flash lights up your finger and the app reads your pulse from the color of your skin. You do it again after the session, and you see how far your heart has come down.

It's the same principle as fingertip pulse oximeters. At rest, the gap with a reference sensor is a few beats. Enough to compare a before and an after, not to give a medical figure. No image is kept: each frame becomes an average color, then disappears.

Sessions without sound

Six sessions of one to three minutes, eyes open, guided by text and by vibrations that set the pace of your breath. Standing after a run. At the office before a meeting. In the kitchen between two things. On the subway. Before an exam. At night without waking anyone.

I wrote them because most stressful moments come when you can neither close your eyes nor put on headphones.

A slower voice

The voices are being re-recorded. The body of each session will be spoken much more slowly, with a real pause between two instructions. The return, at the end, will go back to a normal pace: it's the signal that you're waking up. And the volume will be the same from one session to the next. The sessions are arriving in the beta one by one.

What your sessions say about you

After a few weeks, the app compares your own sessions with each other. For example: "Your evening sessions bring your heart down 7 bpm more than your afternoon ones." It says nothing as long as the gap stays within a sensor's noise, and each sentence tells you how many sessions it is based on.

Your note, a week later

After a session, you can write one sentence about how you felt. Six to ten days later, it comes back on the home screen, with a question. It's an old principle of sophrology, looking back on what you went through, and a well-established principle of memory: we remember what we recall at intervals.

In the morning, if you wear a watch

The home screen compares your night, your resting heart rate and your heart rate variability with your last fourteen days. It tells you whether your body is rested, as usual, or needs taking it easy. There is no score out of 100. I don't believe a phone app has the measurements to give an honest one.

And smaller things

The third part of the day is now called Remember: one minute to recall, from memory, the idea you learned that morning. Recent and Favorites are at the top of Explore. A home screen widget shows the session of the day. The buttons now share the logo's color.

What stays the same

Sessions stay free. What the app reads from Apple Health is described, line by line, in the privacy policy. Replere is not a medical device.
