Recovering after a marathon or trail race

For your body, the finish line is not the end of the race. What you do in the hours and days that follow shapes how well you bounce back.

Updated October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

At the finish

  • Keep walking for a few minutes, even slowly.
  • Cover up: you cool down very fast once you stop.
  • Drink and eat whatever goes down: that's what the finish-line food station is for.
  • Take two minutes to sit down and breathe slowly. It's often the first moment you truly take in what you've just done.

That evening

A real meal, water, and as long a night as you can get. Sleep is the most effective recovery tool you have. Post-race excitement can delay it: soft lighting, screens put away, and a moment to let your body relax once you're in bed all help you switch off.

The days after

  • Move gently: walking, an easy bike ride, swimming. Light movement helps more than staying still.
  • Ease back into running, listening to how you feel rather than following a rigid plan.
  • Keep an eye on your fatigue: a resting heart rate higher than usual, a drop in heart rate variability, no motivation for anything. These are signs to be patient.

Don't ignore these. Pain in one spot that keeps getting worse, swelling, very dark urine, feeling faint or unwell: see a doctor rather than waiting it out.
