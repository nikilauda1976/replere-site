Recovering after a run: the ten minutes that follow

Your session ends when you stop your watch. Your body takes longer to come down. What you do right after matters.

Updated October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Why your body stays wound up

During exercise, your "action" nervous system (sympathetic) speeds up your heart and breathing. When you stop, your "rest" system (parasympathetic) takes over again, but not all at once: your heart rate drops fast in the first minute, then much more slowly.

This return to calm is what we call recovery. You can't speed it up at will. But you can avoid slowing it down (standing around tense, jumping straight onto your phone) and help it along with a few simple steps.

A four-step routine

1 · 3 to 5 minutes

Walk

Don't stop dead. A few minutes of walking lets your heart and breathing come down gradually.

2 · 2 minutes

Breathe slowly

Sit down or stay standing, shoulders low. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds, then breathe out slowly for 6 seconds. That's six breaths a minute. If you feel dizzy, go back to normal breathing.

3 · 1 minute

Relax, one area at a time

Jaw, shoulders, hands, belly, legs: bring your attention to each area and let it loosen as you breathe out.

4 · 30 seconds

Note how you feel

Calmer, the same, more tense? One note per session is enough. After a few weeks, you'll know what really helps you.

Then come the basics: drink, eat within the next few hours, sleep. They matter more than any tool.

What the research says

A randomized crossover trial published in 2025 (Clinical Physiology and Functional Imaging) compared breathing at six cycles per minute after exercise with recovery without instructions. Heart rate variability, a marker of the return to rest, came back faster with slow breathing. Read the study

It's an encouraging result, in a small number of people. It doesn't say you'll run faster next time.

With a watch

If your watch shows your heart rate, look at it during the two minutes of breathing: watching the number drop helps many runners settle down. Compare one session with another rather than with other people.
