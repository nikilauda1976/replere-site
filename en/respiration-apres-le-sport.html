Breathing after exercise: three simple techniques

After exercise, your breathing is the only lever on your nervous system that you control directly. Here are three ways to use it, from the simplest to the most structured.

Updated October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

1. Six breaths a minute

Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds, breathe out for 6 seconds. One cycle lasts 10 seconds, so six a minute. This is the pace studied in the 2025 trial on recovery after exercise. Read the study

How long: 2 to 5 minutes. When: right after your session, once you've caught your breath.

2. The longer out-breath

Keep your natural pace on the in-breath, and simply make the out-breath longer, as if you were gently blowing on a candle without putting it out. Useful when you're still out of breath and a set pace feels hard.

How long: 1 to 2 minutes. When: as soon as you stop, while walking.

3. Box breathing

Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, breathe out for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds. Counting keeps your mind busy: this technique is most useful before a big moment (a race start, an exam) rather than in the middle of recovery.

How long: 4 to 6 cycles. When: before a moment that matters.

Precautions

  • If you feel dizzy or get tingling, go back to normal breathing.
  • Don't hold your breath if you have a heart or lung condition without first talking to your doctor.
  • Never while driving, swimming, or in any situation that needs your attention.
