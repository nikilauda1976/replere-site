Stretching after a run: worth it or not?

It's the most common thing runners do at the end of a run. Yet the research is clear on one point: it does almost nothing for muscle soreness.

Updated October 13, 2026 · 4 min read

What the Cochrane review says

A Cochrane review, the gold standard for summarizing clinical trials, pooled the randomized trials on stretching and muscle soreness. The conclusion: stretching before, after, or both before and after exercise does not meaningfully reduce soreness in healthy adults. Read the review

When done after exercise, the effect measured the next day is about one point on a 100-point pain scale. In other words, you wouldn't notice it. The trials' results are highly consistent with one another.

So should you stop stretching?

Not necessarily. This review answers just one question: soreness. If stretching feels good, helps you settle down, or keeps you flexible, there's no reason to stop. Just go gently and don't push tired muscles.

But if you only have five minutes after a workout, this probably isn't where they count most.

What helps more in the minutes after

1

Walk for a few minutes

Instead of stopping dead, let your heart rate and breathing come down gradually.

2

Breathe slowly

Six breaths a minute, with each exhale longer than the inhale. A 2025 trial found a faster return to resting levels after exercise with this pace. Read the study

3

Drink, eat, sleep

The basics, which matter more than anything you do at the finish.
