Sophrology and running

What it is, what the research honestly says about it, and how to use it around your training.

Updated October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

What sophrology is

Sophrology is a method created in the 1960s by the neuropsychiatrist Alfonso Caycedo. It combines three tools: breathing, muscle relaxation, and visualization (picturing a situation in a positive way). It's widespread in France, especially in preparing athletes.

Three moments for a runner

After exercise

Slow breathing and relaxation to help your body come back down.

Before a race

Box breathing and visualizing the course to channel pre-race nerves.

In the evening

Relaxing one area of the body at a time, to sleep better after a big workout.

What the research shows, and what it doesn't

The tools of sophrology are well studied. In a 2025 trial, slow breathing sped up the return to resting levels after exercise. Read the study In a meta-analysis of 31 randomized trials, progressive muscle relaxation improved sleep quality. Read the study

Sophrology as a discipline has been studied much less: few trials, small ones, with mixed results. The SOPHROCARE trial, for example, found no difference on its primary outcome. Read the study

Put simply: the techniques work, but the label doesn't add any proof. And sophrology doesn't treat anything; it's a form of support.

Choosing a sophrologist

The title of sophrologist isn't regulated in France: anyone can use it. Before booking, ask what training they completed, how many hours it involved, and whether they're used to working with athletes. A good sophrologist will refer you to a doctor if what you need is medical care.
