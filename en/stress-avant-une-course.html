Managing stress before a race

Sweaty hands, heart racing in the start corral: nerves are your body's normal response to something at stake. The goal isn't to get rid of them, but to keep them at a useful level.

Updated October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Why we get stressed

Before a big event, your "action" nervous system gets your body ready: your heart speeds up, your breathing moves higher in your chest, your muscles tense. A bit of this activation helps you perform. Too much, and you go out too fast, your legs tighten up, your mind races.

Three tools to try in training first

The day before and that morning

Visualize the course

Close your eyes and run through the race: the start, the first kilometer at the right pace, the hill, the hard moment and how you get through it. Picture concrete situations, not just the finish.

In the start corral

Box breathing

Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. Four to six cycles. Counting keeps your mind busy and slows your breathing.

Just before the start

A cue word

A short word you chose in advance ("smooth," "patient") that you repeat to yourself as you breathe out.

Don't try anything new on race day: these tools work best once they've become habits.

What we know about how well they work

Trials on slow breathing and heart coherence in athletes before competition are encouraging, but they are small and few. They mainly show a drop in how anxious people feel; the effect on performance itself is less established. Think of these tools as a way to be in good shape for the start, not a guarantee of a finish time.
