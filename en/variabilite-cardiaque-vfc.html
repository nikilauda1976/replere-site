Heart rate variability, explained for runners

Your watch talks about “HRV.” Behind those three letters is a simple idea, and a useful tool, as long as you read it the right way.

Updated October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

What it is

Your heart doesn't beat like a metronome. Heart rate variability is the variation in time between one heartbeat and the next. Read the overview At rest, in a relaxed person, that interval changes constantly, notably in rhythm with breathing. When the body is under strain, it becomes more regular.

An HRV higher than your usual generally reflects a nervous system that's more available for recovery. A clearly lower HRV suggests a body still busy handling a load: training, stress, a bad night, illness.

What makes it change

  • The previous day's training, especially if it was long or hard.
  • Sleep, alcohol, a late meal.
  • Stress at work or in your personal life.
  • Breathing: slow breathing raises it while you're doing it.

How to use it without getting lost

Rule 1

Compare your numbers with your own, not with other people's

Values vary enormously from one person to another and from one watch to another. Only your own trend matters.

Rule 2

Look at the trend, not the day

A single low reading means nothing. Several days of decline, along with feeling tired, are a cue to ease off.

Rule 3

Measure under the same conditions

Overnight, or in the morning when you wake up, always the same way. A reading taken while walking to the kitchen isn't comparable.

And after exercise?

Right after a workout, HRV is low. That's expected. What matters is how quickly it climbs back up. That's exactly what slow breathing seems to speed up in the 2025 trial on recovery. Read the study
